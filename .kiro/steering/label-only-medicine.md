# Invariant: the label is the law

Medicine storage numbers come only from entries in the validated formulary: AI-proposed, checked by a second model from a different family, then approved by a licensed pharmacist. Nothing else may produce a medicine clock, budget or storage claim.

Sources: `docs/afterglow-project-description.md` sections 7.6 and 11 (loaded via `product.md`) and `docs/afterglow-architecture.md` sections 7, 9, 10.3 and ADR-011 (loaded via `tech.md`).

## Rules
1. **Validated entries only.** The app and the core use a formulary entry only if it has the shape `{ product, labelVersion, condition: unopened|inUse, maxTempC, maxDays, notes, source, approver, approvedAt, signature }` and its signature verifies. Anything else is not in the table.
2. **Not in the table means no clock.** Show "ask your pharmacist", explain why, and offer to photograph the label for the review queue. Do not estimate, extrapolate from a similar product, or use a general rule.
3. **Excursion clock**: consumes the label budget only while the modelled or measured temperature is outside the refrigerated range and within the label's room-temperature allowance. Beyond the allowance the clock stops and the app says "excursion beyond label: consult your pharmacist". It never says "safe" beyond the label and never extends a budget.
4. **AI proposes; it never writes the table or computes a clock.** The proposer and validator use different model families. Every proposal is logged with its inputs, model ids and the deterministic acceptance result. A human pharmacist approves before anything is published.
5. **Only the review pipeline writes `afterglow-formulary/validated/**`.** Agents write proposals to `afterglow-formulary/proposals/**` and review items to `review-queue/**`, never to the validated table. Do not edit validated entries by hand, even to fix a typo; open a proposal.
6. **Provenance is shown.** Medicine screens display the label excerpt, label version, approver and approval date, and the sentence that the figures are derived from the label.
7. **"Ask Afterglow"** may phrase deterministic lookups with citations and must refuse medicine questions outside the validated corpus.
8. **Not medical advice.** No diagnosis, no dosing guidance, no "safe to use" claims. The user's pharmacist always has the last word.
9. **Food is separate.** Food discard times come from the USDA FoodKeeper snapshot and regional equivalents, not from the formulary, and follow fail-closed.

## When writing or reviewing code
- Properties: a clock never exceeds its budget; an unvalidated or unsigned entry never yields a clock; the clock is monotonic while outside range.
- Reject a change that hard-codes a storage temperature or duration for a named drug, calls a model to decide a budget, or adds a fallback budget when lookup fails.
- Table and documentation are regenerated by the `formulary-docs-regen` hook; do not let them drift.
